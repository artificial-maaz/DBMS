# Crash Training — One Branch Manager, One Day

Agenda for the first training session, 2026-08-16. Roughly three hours with breaks.

**Goal:** by the end of the day the BM can run a real sale end to end without help, and knows what
to do when he is unsure. Not "has seen every screen" — that is a different and less useful goal.

**Bring:** his own phone, a laptop if he has one, and two or three real bikes' details to enter.

---

## Before he arrives (you, 20 minutes)

- [ ] Create his staff account: Admin → Staff → Onboard. Role **Branch Manager**, his branch.
- [ ] Give him the temporary password on paper, not over WhatsApp.
- [ ] Confirm his branch exists with the correct name, city, address and phone.
- [ ] Print: the **Delivery Process** (both flows) and page one of the **User Manual**.
- [ ] Have one real bike ready to register live, and one real customer scenario in mind.

---

## Session 1 — Getting in and getting oriented (30 min)

1. Sign in on his **phone first**, then install to the home screen. He will use the phone more than
   a laptop; make that the default in his head from minute one.
2. Walk the sidebar top to bottom. Name each group in one sentence. Do not open everything.
3. Dark mode toggle, search box, the bell.
4. **Say the approval rule out loud and let him repeat it back:** money and stock actions wait for
   an owner; customers, visitors and test drives save straight away.

**Check he can:** sign in unaided on a second attempt, and find a customer by phone number.

---

## Session 2 — The counter procedure (45 min)

Do this **before** any data entry. The software is easier once the procedure is clear.

1. Open **Retail → Delivery Process**. Walk the *Cash Sale* flow aloud, one step at a time.
2. Stop hard at the registration fork. Make him say what changes: who keeps the original document,
   and whether it is the original or a photocopy in the envelope.
3. Walk the *Installment Sale* flow. Emphasise what is **kept as security** — original document,
   CNIC copies, utility bill, one key and one remote.
4. State the two absolutes: **never hand over a sales tax invoice**, and **payment before a parts
   order, always**.
5. Give him the printed copy for the counter wall.

**Check he can:** recite what goes in the envelope for both registration answers, without looking.

---

## Session 3 — A real sale, start to finish (60 min)

Use a genuine bike and a genuine walk-in scenario. Let him drive; you narrate.

1. **Register the bike**: Showroom → Inventory → Add Vehicle. Chassis, engine, colour, prices.
2. **Register the customer** from inside New Sale — show him he does not have to leave the page.
3. **Cash sale**: full amount, handover checklist, finish. Show it landing in the Review Queue.
4. **You approve it** from your own login while he watches. Show the invoice appearing, the ledger
   entry, and the bike flipping to sold.
5. **Print the invoice.** Show him the company logo, the branch beneath it, and the handover list on
   the page.
6. Repeat as an **installment sale**: rate card auto-fill, guarantor rows, document checklist.
7. Collect one instalment against it and show the balance falling.

**Check he can:** complete a cash sale unaided while you sit on your hands.

---

## Session 4 — The daily rhythm (30 min)

1. **Retail → Formats & Messages.** Generate the stock report for his branch from live inventory,
   copy it, paste it into the group. This is the moment most managers decide they like the system.
2. Booking message, parts demand, transfer message.
3. Show the printable inter-dealership letter and where the blanks are.
4. **Morning routine:** Dashboard, then Review Queue, then Installment Cases if anything is overdue.
5. **Evening routine:** stock report, cash in hand, anything pending in the queue.

**Check he can:** produce and send the stock report without prompting.

---

## Session 5 — When he is unsure (15 min)

- Errors are written for him, not for a programmer. Read them.
- Nothing is deleted. If something is wrong, do **not** work around it — say so.
- The Audit Log records everything, so mistakes are recoverable and nobody is blamed for admitting
  one quickly.
- Friday closure is enforced on test drives.
- Locked out → contact you, not a colleague.

---

## First week after

- [ ] He enters **every** sale the same day. Backdating exists, but the habit matters more.
- [ ] You check the Review Queue and Audit Log each morning for the first five days.
- [ ] Day 3: ask him to explain the approval rule back to you. If he cannot, revisit it.
- [ ] Day 5: ask what slowed him down. Fix the top item before adding a second branch.
- [ ] Do not onboard anyone else until he can teach it.
